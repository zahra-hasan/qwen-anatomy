Qwen2.5-7B Anatomy

An interactive look inside Qwen2.5 7B Instruct: how its weights are arranged and how it turns text into the next word. Everything here was read from the model file Ollama downloads for qwen2.5:7b (GGUF format, Q4_K_M quantization, 4.68 GB, sha256-2bada8a7…). Every shape, storage type and value on this page comes from that file. Click anything to inspect it.

How Qwen2.5-7B turns "The capital of France is" into its next word, followed through every stage. The scene is built from this model's real shapes and values. Where something is only illustrative, the step says so.

Turn: drag. Move: Shift-drag or right-drag, the arrow buttons, or the arrow keys after clicking the scene. Zoom: scroll, or + and −.

    Words you'll meet

    Each word becomes a list of 3,584 numbers (the residual stream, purple). It passes down through 28 identical layers. Each layer reads the stream, computes an update with its 14 tensors, and adds that update back. Pick a layer on the left, then click a tensor in the diagram.

    Every number in the boxes is this layer's real value. The thin bar at the bottom of each box shows where this layer falls between the lowest and highest of all 28 layers. σ is the spread of the weights, from 8,192 values sampled across each matrix.

    The model has parameters. Click a segment to see what it is. Most of the weight sits in the feed-forward matrices. The norms and biases are only a sliver, but they set the scale of everything flowing through each layer.

    Bytes on disk by storage format

    Q4_K packs 256 weights into 144 bytes (4.5 bits each). Q6_K packs 256 into 210 bytes (6.56 bits). F32 is a plain 4-byte float. Q4_K_M gives the more sensitive tensors Q6_K.

    Each layer has 28 query heads but only 4 key/value heads. Heads 0–6 share key/value head 0, heads 7–13 share head 1, and so on. Colour shows the largest bias value in each head. Some biases are huge (beyond ±10), far larger than a typical weight. Click a cell to highlight its group and open that layer.

    The 28 layers have the same shapes but different values. Pick a measurement to compare layers, and click a bar to open that layer. The first and last layers stand out on almost every measure.
